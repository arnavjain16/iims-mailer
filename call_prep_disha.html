Her topics, and what to know about each

  • Air pollution and climate are linked. The same sources (coal power, industry, vehicles, crop burning) cause both. Policies that cut one often cut the other ("co-benefits").
  • India's air-quality data: CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board) runs the monitoring network. Her Princeton paper analysed this data (2015–2019). Common issues in this field: uneven station coverage, missing data, and whether improvements come from policy or the weather.
  • NCAP (National Clean Air Programme, 2019): India's plan to cut PM pollution in non-attainment cities, with a target of about 40% lower PM10 by 2025–26 compared with 2017.
  • Emission trading for air pollution: Surat's particulate-matter trading scheme (Gujarat, 2019) was the world's first market for particulate pollution. A randomised evaluation found it cut industrial pollution by roughly 20–30% at lower cost. It's a perfect bridge between her two fields.
  • Carbon trading: the earlier PAT scheme (energy-saving certificates) and the new Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (2023). Targets are set as emissions intensity, which links to your greenwashing finding that firms highlight falling intensity while total emissions rise.
  • Global accords: Paris Agreement, NDCs and COPs. India's targets are net zero by 2070 and a 45% cut in emissions intensity of GDP by 2030 (vs 2005).
  • Green growth and entrepreneurship: businesses and start-ups built around cleaner technology (renewables, clean air tech, circular economy).

Her field in five minutes

Emission trading (carbon markets)

A way to cut emissions at the lowest cost. The government sets a limit (a "cap" or an emissions-intensity target), firms get or buy permits or credits, and firms that cut more than needed can sell the extra to firms that cut less. The alternative is a carbon tax (a fixed price per tonne).

  • EU ETS (since 2005): the best-known cap-and-trade market.
  • India: the PAT scheme (from 2012) traded energy-saving certificates among heavy industries. The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS, 2023) is building the Indian Carbon Market, run with the Bureau of Energy Efficiency, with emission-intensity targets for sectors plus an offset route.
  • Good questions in this space: is the price high enough to change behaviour? Are offsets real ("additional")? Is reporting trustworthy? The last one is your greenwashing link.

Climate policy and global accords

  • Paris Agreement (2015): keep warming well below 2°C, aiming for 1.5°C. Each country submits targets called NDCs; negotiations continue at yearly COP meetings.
  • India's commitments: net zero by 2070; by 2030, cut the emissions intensity of GDP by 45% from 2005 levels and get about 50% of electricity capacity from non-fossil sources.
  • Climate and air pollution share sources (coal, vehicles, crop burning). Her Princeton work analysed India's air-pollution data.

Green growth

Growing the economy while cutting emissions and environmental damage (renewables, efficiency, green jobs and green entrepreneurship). In finance this shows up as ESG investing and green bonds. In India, large listed firms report sustainability data in SEBI's BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report), a natural dataset for greenwashing checks.

Words you might hear or use

Greenwashing
Making products or a company look greener than they are.
ESG
Environmental, Social, Governance: how investors score a firm's sustainability.
Cap-and-trade
A limit on emissions plus tradable permits.
Carbon offset / credit
One tonne of CO₂ reduced or avoided elsewhere, sold to someone else.
NDC
A country's climate pledge under the Paris Agreement.
Emissions intensity
Emissions per unit of GDP or output, not total emissions.
CBDC / e-rupee
Digital currency issued by the central bank (RBI).
MDR
Merchant Discount Rate: the fee a merchant pays on a digital payment. Zero on UPI.
DCF
Value = today's value of future cash flows.
WACC
A firm's average cost of money (equity plus debt), used as the discount rate.
CAPM
Cost of equity = risk-free rate + beta × market risk premium.
IRR
The return rate at which a project's value (NPV) is exactly zero.
ROCE
Return on capital employed: operating profit ÷ capital used.
DuPont
Splits a return ratio into margin × asset turnover (× leverage) to see what changed.
Goodwill
The part of an acquisition price above the value of the assets bought.
Stationary series
A time series whose mean and variance don't drift over time.
